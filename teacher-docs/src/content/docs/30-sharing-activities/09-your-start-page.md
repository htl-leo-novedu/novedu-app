When you sign in, the start page gives you a quick look at the codes **you** created: what needs your attention, how much they were used in the last 30 days, which activities your students use most, and the badges you have earned. Other teachers' codes never appear here, and the page never names a student.

## Needs you

Three counters across the top tell you whether anything needs you:

- **Closing soon**: codes whose time window ends within the next three days. A code that closes today is marked in red.
- **Open reports**: student reports on your codes that nobody has marked resolved yet. The list shows which codes have them, and a link takes you to the **Reports** page.
- **Never used**: codes you created more than a week ago that are open but nobody has used yet. Perhaps the link never reached the class.

Select a counter to see up to five of its codes; select a code to open its statistics page. A counter with nothing in it turns into a green "all clear" message.

## Last 30 days

Six numbers sum up your codes over the last 30 days:

- **Live codes**: codes students can open right now.
- **Identified students**: everyone who has ever taken part in your per-user activities, or picked up a key for one of your coding activities. Students in anonymous activities can't be counted, so the real number may be higher.
- **Conversations**: chats in which a student wrote at least one message.
- **Quiz answers**, **Input tokens** and **Output tokens**: how many quiz answers were graded and how much text the AI models read and wrote.

## Top activities

Your five codes with the most interactions in the last 30 days. An interaction is a chat message, a quiz answer, a saved text, a request from a coding tool, or a graded cloze submission (practice rounds included). Your own test runs count too.

**Outside school hours** shows how much of that happened on weekends, or on weekdays before 8:00 or from 17:00 (Austrian time). A high share suggests students use the activity as homework. School holidays aren't known, so a weekday in the holidays still counts as school time.

For a breakdown by day, model, and kind of activity across the whole installation, select **Usage dashboard** below the list (see [Seeing how a code is used](/30-sharing-activities/02-viewing-usage/)).

## Badges

At the bottom of the page you collect badges for your work with Novedu. Each one shows the day you earned it; the ones you haven't earned yet show what they need and how close your best activity is. New badges are announced by a short line at the top of the page on your next visit.

**Reach**

- **First Code**: share your first activity.
- **Full Toolkit**: share a tutor, a quiz, a writing, a coding and a cloze activity.
- **Small Crowd**, **Full Class**, **Packed House**: 10, 30 or 100 identified students on one activity. Students in anonymous activities can't be counted.
- **Busy**, **Buzzing**, **Hive of Activity**: 100, 1,000 or 5,000 interactions on one activity.

**Authoring**

- **Evergreen**: one activity used in 8 different weeks.
- **Iterator**: save 5 versions of one YAML file.
- **Listener**: resolve 10 student reports, on any code.
- **Homework Hit**: one activity with at least 50 interactions, at least half of them outside school hours.

A badge stays earned even if you later delete the code or reopen a report. Badges are yours alone: other teachers and students never see them.

## Before your first code

If you haven't shared an activity yet, the start page shows a short greeting, a link to this guide, and the badges you can earn, with **First Code** first. The counters and numbers appear once you have created a code.
